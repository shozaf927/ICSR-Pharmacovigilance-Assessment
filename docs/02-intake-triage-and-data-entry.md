# Case Intake, Triage and Data Entry

## Intake channels
Potential cases can arrive through safety mailboxes, call centres, medical information, sales representatives, literature surveillance, patient support programmes, clinical operations, regulators, business partners and digital sources. Every channel needs a documented, prompt transfer process to pharmacovigilance.

## Intake data
Capture the original unaltered source, receipt date/time and time zone, sender/source type, reporter details, patient details, suspect and concomitant products, event verbatim, onset and outcome, seriousness indicators, treatment, relevant history, attachments, translation status and privacy restrictions.

## Triage flags
Escalate possible death, life-threatening events, hospitalisation/prolongation, disability, congenital anomaly, important medical events, pregnancy exposure/outcomes, paediatric cases, overdose, medication error, misuse, abuse, off-label use, occupational exposure, lack of efficacy, product quality issue or falsified product concern. A triage flag is not a final medical conclusion.

## Duplicate search
Compare patient identifiers/age/sex, reporter, product, event, country, dates, source and chronology. Do not merge cases merely because the same drug and event appear. Preserve provenance and follow database SOPs for linking duplicate records.

## Data-entry rules
- Preserve verbatim terms separately from coded terms.
- Do not turn assumptions into facts.
- Record date precision and unknowns transparently.
- Capture product strength, dose, route, frequency, indication, start/stop dates and batch/lot where available.
- Record event onset/end independently from drug dates.
- Record dechallenge and rechallenge explicitly; “not performed” is not “negative”.
- Record outcome at each report stage and update on follow-up.
- Distinguish suspect from concomitant products.

## Privacy
Use only necessary patient identifiers. Public training repositories must contain synthetic or properly de-identified information only. Follow applicable privacy laws, retention rules, access controls and secure transfer procedures.
