# ICSR Fundamentals: Validity, Day 0 and Lifecycle

## Definition
An Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR) is a structured report of one or more suspected adverse reactions/events in an individual associated with medicinal product exposure. ICH E2B(R3) specifies electronic data elements and message structure for ICSR exchange.

## Four minimum criteria
A valid report generally requires all four:
1. **Identifiable patient** — sufficient to distinguish a real individual (e.g., initials, age, sex or patient identifier).
2. **Identifiable reporter** — distinguishable source, ideally with a contact route.
3. **Suspect medicinal product** — at least one product suspected in association with the event.
4. **Adverse event/reaction** — at least one identifiable event.

Dose, batch number, exact onset date, complete history and outcome are desirable but are not additional minimum validity criteria. A valid but incomplete case should be processed and followed up, not held until every field is complete.

## Day 0
Day 0 is the date the organisation first receives the minimum information required for a valid case under the applicable framework and SOP. It is not the date of database entry, medical review or receipt of a later discharge summary. Preserve the original source, receipt timestamp, time zone and recipient function. Escalate uncertain Day 0 decisions.

## Terminology
- **AE:** untoward medical occurrence after exposure; causality need not be established.
- **ADR:** noxious and unintended response for which a causal relationship is at least suspected, according to applicable definition.
- **SAE:** event meeting one or more regulatory seriousness criteria.
- **SUSAR:** suspected, unexpected, serious adverse reaction in a clinical-trial context.

## Sources and case types
Sources include spontaneous HCP/consumer reports, literature, regulatory authorities, solicited programmes, patient support programmes, clinical studies and digital sources. ICH E2D(R1) updates post-approval safety data concepts and newer sources; regional implementation remains relevant.

## Lifecycle
1. Receive and preserve source.
2. Validate four criteria and establish Day 0.
3. Search for duplicates.
4. Triage seriousness and special situations.
5. Enter data and code products/events.
6. Medical review and causality assessment.
7. Assess expectedness against applicable RSI.
8. Determine reportability and due date.
9. QC, submit and capture acknowledgement.
10. Follow up, reconcile and maintain the case.

**Principle:** completeness is not validity. Do not delay a valid case solely because follow-up information is missing.
