# Causality Assessment: WHO-UMC and Naranjo

Causality estimates whether a product may have caused an event. Consider chronology, pharmacology, dechallenge, rechallenge, alternative causes, concomitant products, medical history, objective findings and report quality.

## WHO-UMC categories
| Category | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Certain | Plausible timing; disease/other drugs do not explain event; plausible withdrawal response; pharmacologically/phenomenologically definitive; rechallenge satisfactory if needed |
| Probable/Likely | Reasonable timing; unlikely alternative explanation; clinically reasonable withdrawal response; rechallenge not required |
| Possible | Reasonable timing, but disease/other drugs could explain event; withdrawal information may be unclear |
| Unlikely | Timing makes relationship improbable; other explanations plausible |
| Conditional/Unclassified | More data needed or under examination |
| Unassessable/Unclassifiable | Insufficient/contradictory information cannot be supplemented or verified |

WHO-UMC is a clinical judgement framework, not a point score.

## Naranjo scale
Ten questions assess previous reports, temporal sequence, withdrawal response, rechallenge, alternative causes, placebo, toxic concentrations, dose response, previous similar reaction and objective evidence.
- Definite: ≥9
- Probable: 5–8
- Possible: 1–4
- Doubtful: ≤0

Use “do not know” when information is unavailable; do not automatically score unknown as “No”. Rechallenge may be unethical or clinically inappropriate. Naranjo does not replace medical judgement or regulatory assessment.

## Worked Naranjo score: fictional anaphylaxis
| Question | Answer | Score | Rationale |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Previous conclusive reports? | Yes | +1 | Recognised hypersensitivity reaction; verify product information |
| Event after administration? | Yes | +2 | Onset ~15 minutes after dose |
| Improvement after withdrawal/specific antagonist? | Yes | +1 | Product stopped; improvement after epinephrine and other treatment |
| Recurrence on readministration? | Unknown/not performed | 0 | No rechallenge |
| Alternative causes? | Unknown | 0 | Not fully excluded |
| Recurrence with placebo? | Unknown/not performed | 0 | Not done |
| Toxic concentration? | Unknown/not measured | 0 | Not measured |
| Dose response? | Unknown | 0 | No dose comparison |
| Similar prior reaction? | Not reported | 0 | No prior event documented |
| Objective evidence? | Yes | +1 | Hypotension, desaturation, wheezing, urticaria and diagnosis |
| **Total** |  | **5 — Probable** |  |

The previous-reports answer must be supported by reliable product/class evidence. Unknown information scores zero, not an assumed negative.

## WHO-UMC result for the fictional case
**Possible (provisional).** Timing is compelling, but alternative causes are not adequately excluded and improvement followed multiple interventions, so independent dechallenge effect is difficult to isolate. Additional records may change the assessment.

WHO-UMC and Naranjo may differ because their criteria and weighting differ. Explain the evidence; do not force agreement. A valid case can require processing even when causality remains uncertain.
