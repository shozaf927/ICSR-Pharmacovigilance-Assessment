# Seriousness, Severity and Expectedness

These are three separate assessments and must be documented independently.

## Seriousness: regulatory criteria
An event is serious if it results in or meets an applicable criterion:
- Death.
- Life-threatening at the time (actual immediate risk of death, not merely a hypothetical possibility).
- Inpatient hospitalisation or prolongation of existing hospitalisation.
- Persistent or significant disability/incapacity.
- Congenital anomaly/birth defect.
- Other medically important event that may jeopardise the patient or require intervention to prevent a listed outcome.

Medical judgement is needed for important medical events. Emergency department treatment alone does not automatically establish inpatient hospitalisation.

## Severity: clinical intensity
Severity describes intensity, commonly mild, moderate or severe; a scale such as CTCAE may be used when appropriate.
- Mild: transient symptoms, little/no intervention, minimal functional impact.
- Moderate: intervention needed or some activity limitation.
- Severe: marked symptoms, substantial intervention or major functional limitation.

A severe headache is not automatically serious. A mild-intensity event can be serious if it causes inpatient hospitalisation or another regulatory outcome.

## CTCAE
CTCAE is used in oncology and clinical research settings, not automatically for every post-marketing report. If required, use the protocol-specified version, exact event term and full grade definition. Do not assign a grade from the word “severe” alone. This project does not assign a CTCAE grade.

## Expectedness
Assess against the applicable Reference Safety Information (RSI): usually the current approved local SmPC/Prescribing Information for a marketed product or the Investigator’s Brochure for a trial, as specified by applicable rules. Verify product, formulation, route, population, country, version/effective date, event specificity and severity. A broad listed term may not cover a more specific or severe manifestation. Record the source and rationale.

## Worked fictional case
- Seriousness: serious; life-threatening features and inpatient admission are reported.
- Severity: severe clinical intensity.
- Expectedness: assumed expected solely for this exercise because the hypothetical RSI is assumed to list anaphylaxis/severe hypersensitivity. A real conclusion requires the exact current local RSI.

## Why the distinction matters
Seriousness drives regulatory reporting criteria; severity describes clinical intensity; expectedness contributes to SUSAR determination in trials and other regional decisions. Causality is separate from all three.
