# Case Processing, Narrative, QC and Follow-up

## Narrative structure
1. Patient demographics and relevant history.
2. Product, indication, dose, route and treatment dates.
3. Concomitant medications/exposures.
4. Event onset and presentation.
5. Investigations/objective findings.
6. Treatment and action taken with suspect product.
7. Clinical course, outcome and discharge.
8. Reporter/physician opinion, if available.
9. Missing information and follow-up status.

Keep it chronological and concise. Distinguish source facts from company assessment; do not speculate.

## Follow-up questions for the fictional case
- Exact dose and symptom onset date/time.
- Prior beta-lactam exposure and allergy history.
- Full medication, food and other exposure history.
- Emergency and discharge summaries.
- Exact BP, oxygen saturation, respiratory findings and treatment times.
- Airway intervention, vasopressors, ICU care or duration of admission.
- Tryptase results/timing, if clinically obtained.
- Outcome and sequelae.
- Product brand, manufacturer, batch/lot, expiry and source.
- Applicable local RSI version.
Do not delay initial reporting while awaiting follow-up if criteria are already met.

## QC checklist
### Validity and chronology
- [ ] Four minimum criteria documented.
- [ ] Day 0 supported by receipt evidence.
- [ ] Initial and follow-up dates distinguished.
- [ ] Duplicate search completed.

### Patient, reporter and product
- [ ] Patient data sufficient and minimised.
- [ ] Reporter/source identifiable.
- [ ] Suspect and concomitant products distinguished.
- [ ] Dose, route, indication and dates checked.

### Event and assessment
- [ ] Verbatim retained.
- [ ] MedDRA/WHODrug coding verified against current release.
- [ ] Seriousness criteria and evidence documented.
- [ ] Severity documented separately.
- [ ] Expectedness assessed against correct RSI.
- [ ] Causality rationale documented.
- [ ] Outcome and action taken recorded.

### Reporting
- [ ] Source type and region identified.
- [ ] Deadline calculated from correct Day 0.
- [ ] Narrative and structured fields consistent.
- [ ] Medical review/QC completed.
- [ ] Acknowledgement/reconciliation recorded.
- [ ] Follow-up assigned.

## Common errors
- Equating severe with serious.
- Waiting for all records before starting the clock.
- Inferring diagnoses from symptoms or labs.
- Using wrong-country/outdated RSI.
- Calling post-marketing cases SUSARs.
- Scoring unknown Naranjo answers as “No”.
- Treating no rechallenge as negative rechallenge.
- Inventing dictionary codes or losing source traceability.
- Publishing identifiable patient data.
