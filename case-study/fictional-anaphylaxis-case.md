# Fictional Case: Suspected Anaphylaxis After Amoxicillin/Clavulanate

> All data are synthetic and for training only. No real patient, reporter, company, brand or submission is represented.

## Source report
On 12 November 2026, a marketing authorisation holder's pharmacovigilance mailbox receives a spontaneous report from a hospital physician in Maharashtra, India.

The physician reports that a 34-year-old female patient (fictional initials A.B.) was prescribed amoxicillin/clavulanate 625 mg immediate-release oral tablet for acute bacterial sinusitis. Approximately 15 minutes after taking the first reported dose, she developed widespread itchy wheals, lip/tongue swelling, wheezing and difficulty breathing. At emergency assessment, blood pressure was 78/46 mmHg and oxygen saturation was 89% on room air. The emergency physician documented anaphylaxis.

The patient received intramuscular epinephrine, oxygen, intravenous fluids, antihistamine and corticosteroid treatment. The suspect medicine was stopped. She was admitted for inpatient observation and discharged after approximately 24 hours with symptoms resolved and no reported sequelae at initial report.

The reporter states no known drug allergy was recorded in the available chart. Full prior antibiotic exposure, concomitant medication history, laboratory details and discharge summary are not yet available. No rechallenge was performed.
